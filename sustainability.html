Closing Note

How NLP Can Contribute to Society and Sustainable Development

Every concept in this repository — tokenizing a sentence, weighting a word by TF-IDF, embedding a word by its context — is ultimately a tool for letting more people be understood by a machine, and more machines be understood by people. That framing connects Week 1's technical content directly to sustainable development, particularly the goals of quality education, reduced inequality, and good health and wellbeing.

Language accessibility is the clearest link. Text-to-speech and speech-to-text systems, built on the same tokenization and language-modelling pipeline covered here, let visually impaired users read the news aloud or let a person with a speech impairment communicate through typed text converted to natural speech. Real-time captioning at the same layer opens lectures, meetings, and broadcasts to Deaf and hard-of-hearing audiences.

Regional and low-resource language technology matters because most NLP research and pretrained models still concentrate on a small set of high-resource languages such as English. Techniques covered in this repository — FastText's sub-word embeddings, in particular — were designed to make morphologically rich and lower-resource languages tractable without needing web-scale corpora, narrowing the gap between speakers of major and minority languages in translation, search, and voice assistants.

Educational inclusion follows from the same tools: automatic summarization can condense a dense textbook chapter for a struggling reader, grammar-correction systems built on language models give students immediate feedback outside the classroom, and machine translation lets a learner study material originally written in a language they do not yet read fluently.

Assistive technologies extend this further — predictive-text keyboards built on statistical and neural language models reduce the physical effort needed to type for users with motor disabilities, and simplified-language generation can rephrase complex official text for readers with cognitive disabilities or limited literacy.

Citizen services benefit when government helplines and public information portals use chatbots and multilingual search to answer questions in a citizen's own language and script, at any hour, reducing the friction between people and the services meant to serve them. Applied deliberately — and audited for the biases that word embeddings and language models are known to inherit from their training data — NLP can be one of the more direct technical routes from a research lab to a more accessible, equitable, and informed society.
